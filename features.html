Everything for your medical, in one private place

A privacy-first app for iPhone, iPad, and Mac that keeps your records, your medical certificate dates (first class, second class, or third class), your Special Issuance paperwork, and your MedXPress and BasicMed prep organized, so you walk into every exam ready.

The problem is usually the paperwork, not your health

When a pilot runs into trouble with their FAA medical, it's usually not their health. It's the documentation. In the Federal Air Surgeon's own words, "most of these denials resulted because of a failure of the applicants to provide sufficient information." And it's common: about 1 in 16 certified airmen fly on a Special Issuance, and nearly 1 in 5 past age 65.

The FAA says so itself: in its April 2025 review, the agency named "incomplete information on airmen's submissions" a top challenge, and noted Special Issuances have roughly doubled since 2019. Getting the paperwork complete before you submit is the part this app helps with.

Sources: FAA Federal Air Surgeon · FAA 2024 Aerospace Medical Certification Statistical Handbook (Oct 2025) · FAA "Improving the Medical Process" (Apr 2025).

MedXPress autofillGuardian Pro

Fills the parts of the FAA form you'd otherwise retype every cycle.

On iPhone, iPad and Mac it's one tap: Fill MedXPress opens the FAA's MedXPress site right inside the app, with no Safari setup. Prefer Safari? The extension still works. On a Windows PC, the Chrome or Edge extension does it. Either way, it fills MedXPress Items 1 to 19 straight into the live form: your application details, medications (Item 17), every Item 18 answer, Yes and No, with the explanations you saved, and doctor visits (Item 19), so you don't retype the same information every renewal. You review every entry and submit it yourself. The autofill sends nothing to me. Your first MedXPress autofill is free for 7 days from your first Fill, in the app or in Safari. After that, autofill every renewal is part of Guardian Pro. On a Windows PC, the Chrome and Edge extension needs Guardian Pro from the start.

CACI pathwayGuardian Pro

Opens the FAA's CACI Worksheet right in the app when one of your Item 18 Yes answers matches a condition on the FAA's CACI list.

If one of your Item 18 Yes answers matches a condition the FAA covers with a CACI Worksheet (hypertension, asthma, hypothyroidism, prediabetes, retained kidney stones, and others), the app points you to that worksheet. It's the FAA's own document for that condition. With Guardian Pro it opens right in the app, offline, to read, share, or print. Without Guardian Pro you still get the FAA citation and a link to it on faa.gov. The app never says whether you'll be issued. That's decided by your AME and the FAA.

My Documents & Share with your AMEGuardian Pro

Scan, import, or share a PDF or photo, keep it organized, then bundle what you choose into one PDF and send it to your AME from your phone. The bundle is built on-device.

Keep lab results, letters, and scans in your own library, then choose exactly what to bundle into one clean PDF for your AME. It's built on your device and sent only when you send it.

Lab results, physician letters, test reports, your Special Issuance authorization. They arrive as PDFs and paper from every direction. Bring them all into one place: scan a paper letter, import a PDF or photo you already have (a result your doctor emailed, or one you saved from your patient portal), or share one straight in from Mail or Files. The app keeps them organized, filed on their own or linked to the Special Issuance condition they belong to.

When it's time for your exam, bundle what you choose into one clean PDF and send it to your AME right from your phone: your certificate or BasicMed dates, medications, visits, Special Issuance, and the documents you've saved. You pick what goes in; it's built on your device and only leaves when you hit share.

Everything stays on your device and your own iCloud. Nothing is uploaded to me.

FAA reference search

Ask a plain question and get the FAA's own answer, with the citation and a link to the source.

Type something like “how long is a first class medical” or the name of a medication, and the app searches everything it carries from the FAA: medication status, the thresholds your readings are measured against, certificate durations, BasicMed, CACI conditions, Special Issuance requirements, the Item 18 questions, and where to mail documents. FAA results are quoted from published FAA material and cited to it, with the date that entry was last verified and a link to the FAA's own page, so you can read the original yourself. Plain-language definitions of aviation-medical terms are listed separately under “Definitions” and labelled as my words, not the FAA's. Searching runs entirely on your device and never touches your own records. It's free, and it reports what the FAA published without telling you what it means for you.

Now on Windows

Keep your records on your iPhone. Fill out MedXPress on your PC.

There's no Windows app. Your records live in Pilot Medical Guardian on your iPhone, iPad, or Mac. On your Windows PC, with Guardian Pro, a small extension for Chrome or Edge reads them and fills MedXPress Items 1 to 19: your application details, medications, every Item 18 answer and your doctor visits. You review every page and submit it yourself.

  1. Get the app on your iPhone or iPad (or Mac) and enter your records there.
  2. Add the extension to Chrome or Edge on your PC.
  3. Sign in with the same Apple Account, open MedXPress, and click Fill.

Sign in with your Apple Account

The same one you use for the app. The extension reads your records from your own iCloud, read-only, and keeps them only in that browser's memory. After 30 minutes the extension won't use them and deletes them the next time you open it or use it to fill MedXPress. Clicking Clear my records or quitting the browser removes them right away. Nothing is sent to me.

Or open a file

On a work computer, or with Advanced Data Protection on? Send to a computer in the app on your iPhone or iPad makes an encrypted file and a code instead.

Why I built this

I'm a pilot at a major U.S. airline. Over the years I watched friends, healthy and capable aviators, get blindsided not by their health, but by the paperwork around it: a renewal that slipped, a Special Issuance requirement that got missed, a MedXPress answer that didn't match last cycle's. So I built the tool I wanted: private by design, grounded in the FAA's own published rules, and built so that no one but you can ever see your data. It keeps you organized; your AME still makes every certification decision.
