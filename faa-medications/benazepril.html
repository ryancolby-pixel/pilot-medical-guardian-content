Benazepril (Lotensin) and the FAA medical

Lotensin · ACE inhibitor (hypertension)

FAA documents updated Mar 23, 2023; Sep 30, 2026; Apr 13, 2022Checked Oct 6, 2026FAA source

Blood pressure: 7-day ground trial when starting; CACI on 3 or fewer drugs.

FAA antihypertensive page: Angiotensin converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors are listed under "ACCEPTABLE HTN Medications", "(when certification criteria are met)".
"Seven-day (7) no fly/ground trial is required when starting a new hypertension (HTN) medication to verify no side effects."
"AME should issue (if otherwise qualified) if the airman is on 3 or fewer medications"
Item 36 table: CACI when "Treated with 3 or fewer* acceptable medications", counting each component ("lisinopril/HCTZ is 2 medications").
DEFER, "Submit the information to the FAA for a possible Special Issuance", when "Treated with 4 or more* acceptable medications", or if "HTN is clinically uncontrolled", "Unacceptable medications are used" or "Side effects are present".
CACI worksheet: office blood pressure "Less than or equal to 155 systolic and 95 diastolic".
The AME reviews a Clinical Progress Note from a visit "no more than 90 days prior to the AME exam", unless the AME can confirm "stable clinical blood pressure control on the current antihypertensive medication for at least 7 days".
How often: annually for first or second class, and with each required exam for third class.

Source: FAA Antihypertensive, updated September 30, 2026; Item 36 Hypertension table, updated March 23, 2023; CACI Hypertension Worksheet, updated April 13, 2022.

That is what the FAA publishes, not a determination about you.
